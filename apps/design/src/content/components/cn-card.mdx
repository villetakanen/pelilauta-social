CnCard presents one independently meaningful subject. Its required title gives every
preview a stable identity, while optional cover, noun, supporting content, and actions
build the amount of context a reader needs before following it.

## Example

<Composition themes caption="A basic CnCard in Light and Dark">
  <CnCard title="Aamunkoin vartijat" description="A concise preview with the default elevation.">
    The next game is on Thursday.
  </CnCard>
</Composition>

```svelte

<CnCard
  title="Aamunkoin vartijat"

>
  The next game is on Thursday.
</CnCard>
```

## Props

| Prop | Type | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `title` | `string` | Required CnCard heading. |
| `description` | `string` | Secondary text after the heading. |
| `elevation` | `0 \| 1 \| 2 \| 3 \| 4` | Shared surface level. Defaults to `1`. |
| `href` | `string` | Links the title and, when present, the decorative cover. |
| `cover` | `string` | Decorative cover image URL. |
| `srcset` | `string` | Native responsive-image candidates used with `cover`. |
| `sizes` | `string` | Native responsive-image sizing hints used with `cover`. |
| `noun` | `string` | Decorative Icon noun, small by the title or large over a cover. |
| `notify` | `boolean` | Draws the information flag. Defaults to `false`. |
| `alert` | `boolean` | Draws the warning flag above notification. Defaults to `false`. |
| `eyebrow` | `Snippet` | Caption content before the title. |
| `children` | `Snippet` | Concise supporting content after the description. |
| `actions` | `Snippet` | Links and controls anchored at the bottom edge. |

## Destinations

The article remains passive when `href` is supplied. The title becomes the keyboard and
assistive-technology destination; a cover points to the same location for pointer users
without adding another focus stop. Independent links and controls remain operable in the
eyebrow, body, and action region.

## Elevation and indicators

CnCard composes the shared elevation levels, keeping their background and shadow model in
one place. Notification and alert are visual flags; the text supplied by a consumer
still communicates what each state means.

At elevation 0, CnCard adds the standard one-pixel border so its edge remains visible
against the application ground plane. Elevation 4 uses the high-contrast text role on
the primary background.

### Setting a flag on the client

Both flags are also a class hook on the card root, for a state that is only known
after the page arrives — an unread thread, an item that needs attention once a session
resolves. Toggle `has-notify` or `has-alert` on the `.cn-card` element instead of
re-rendering the card:

```ts
const card = document.querySelector('#thread-card-abc123 .cn-card')
card?.classList.toggle('has-notify', unread)
```

`cn-card`, `has-notify` and `has-alert` are part of the public contract for this
reason. The flag rules read the classes and nothing else, so a listing that renders
its cards on the server and hydrates none of them can still raise one triangle. The
flag fades in, so a late state does not snap into place. Give the card root a handle
you can query — the surrounding element is usually enough, since CnCard takes no `id`
prop.

The flag itself belongs to [Surface](/base/surface): a card and a listing row show the
same signal, so CnCard composes those states rather than drawing its own.

## Visual identity

A noun identifies the subject without repeating its name to assistive technology. It
sits beside the title until a cover gives it a larger upper-right field. Covers crop to
16:9 and carry the primary tint in both colour schemes.

<Composition themes caption="Noun, cover, failed cover, and linked cover with noun">
  <CardSpecimens group="identity" />
</Composition>

A cover that cannot load falls back to the system cover artwork, so a deleted or moved
image leaves the card's proportions, tint and noun intact rather than an empty 16:9
band. The third specimen above points at a URL that does not resolve, so what it shows
is the real fallback rather than a picture of one — which is also why it resembles the
working covers beside it: they are the same artwork. Pass no `cover` at all and CnCard
renders no cover region, which stays the way to say a subject has no image.

The artwork sits behind the image rather than replacing it on error, so it needs no
script and appears in a CnCard that is never hydrated — every specimen on this page is
server-rendered. It also fills the moment before a lazily loaded cover arrives. The
trade is that a cover with transparency composites over the artwork rather than over
the card surface.

## Composition regions

Use the eyebrow for a category, channel, edition, or system. Keep body content to one to
three short paragraphs. Actions accept links and controls in a neutral container and
stay at the bottom when neighboring CnCards contain more text.

## Narrow containers

CnCard establishes inline-size containment, so supplied content adapts to the
CnCard's width rather than the viewport's. The title is the exception: it keeps
Typography's h4 size, weight, line height, and tracking at every container width,
preserving the v20 card hierarchy in a sidebar or compact listing. The heading occupies
at most two rendered lines.

## When to use it

- Preview one subject that has a title and a bounded amount of supporting content.
- Compose independent destinations or controls around one primary subject destination.

Use [Surface](/base/surface) for untitled boxes and workflow-scale content. The
containing layout defines CnCard width and spacing between CnCards.

## Accessibility

CnCard renders an `article` with an h4 and no implicit whole-CnCard control. Place it where
h4 is valid in the surrounding heading hierarchy. Covers, nouns, and indicators are
supplementary visuals; consumers communicate their meaning in text or another
accessible state.

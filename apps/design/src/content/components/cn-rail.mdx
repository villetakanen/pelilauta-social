Use CnRail to hold the destinations a reader reaches from wherever they are in the
application.

## When to use it

An entry inside it is an ordinary [Chrome Action](/base/chrome-actions) — an anchor or a
button carrying an Icon and a label — and the rail states none of its measurements.

Mount it for the application's own persistent destinations. For a single page's own
contextual action, use a [floating action
button](/base/links-actions-buttons#floating-action-buttons-fab) instead.

## Example

This site's own navigation, beside this page, is a `CnRail`, at real scale: this is what
it looks like where an application mounts one. The frame below holds a second
one, at the same width band, so you do not have to leave this paragraph to try its
trigger, a mounted [CnRailAction](/components/cn-rail-action) — click it and watch the
rail move between its two widths, named after Material
3's own vocabulary for the same control: expanded, showing every entry's label, and
collapsed, its icons alone. The trigger's own glyph turns with it, drawing `=` collapsed
and `|<` expanded, and turning the same way — counter-clockwise — whichever state it is
leaving.

Beside a page, the rail begins where the application bar's own target ends: the bar
spans the whole inline size and keeps its corner for the page's identity, so only the
main region cedes the rail's width. The bar's mark, the trigger and every entry then
share one vertical axis and one 56px rhythm.

Narrower still, past `--cn-breakpoint-small`, a rail rests absent, reachable through its
trigger alone; asked for there or in the collapsed band, it covers the page over a scrim,
`Escape` closes it and returns focus to its trigger, and `Tab` cannot leave it. This
site's own navigation is that rail: narrow your browser window to see each band, and the
covering state, at real scale.

## Reference

### Props

| Prop | Type | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `label` | `string` | Required, and localised. Names the control and the landmark. |
| `id` | `string` | Distinguishes this rail's controls from another's. Defaults to `cn-rail`; a page mounting more than one rail states its own. |

## Accessibility

The rail renders a `nav` landmark named by `label`. Its trigger is `aria-hidden`; the
toggle beside it carries that name, reports its own checked state, and is the sole
focusable path into the rail. While the rail covers the page, `Escape` closes it and
returns focus to that toggle, and `Tab` cannot move focus outside the rail. An entry
inside a box is an ordinary chrome action, so its accessibility is that capability's,
documented at [Chrome Actions](/base/chrome-actions).

`CnLoader` combines two signals in one element: a ring whose rotation says that work is
in flight, and a static icon at its centre that says what the work is about. The icon is
the same `Icon` every other surface uses, so the loader inherits the noun vocabulary of
the thing it stands in for.

## When to use it

Render `CnLoader` while a region cannot yet show its content, and give it the noun of the
subject being fetched — a reader who sees the fox knows which listing is late. Use the
standalone size when the loader replaces a region, and `inline` when it sits on a text
line or inside a control.

The ring reports that something is happening and never how much of it is done. The design
system publishes no determinate indicator, so a task whose progress is measurable is not
served by this component.

## Variants and states

**Standalone**, the default, is three lines square (72px) and renders as a block. It is
the size for a region that has nothing else in it yet.

**Inline** is one line square (24px) and renders inline, so it sits on a text baseline
beside a label without disturbing the line box. Its icon steps down with it; ring
thickness does not, which keeps the motion visible at the smaller size.

**Any noun** may be passed. The icon resolves through the same tiers as `Icon`, so an
unknown noun renders the missing glyph rather than an empty ring.

## Composition

A loader that is a direct child of a `<section>` or an `<article>` is centred
horizontally and given one line of margin above and below, by `styles/loader.css` rather
than by the component, so a container gets the treatment without the loader knowing where
it stands.

<Composition themes caption="The standalone loader as a direct child of a section">
  <LoaderSpecimens group="section" />
</Composition>

Any other parent positions the loader itself. A `CnCard` whose subject is still resolving
is the case to know about: its content region is a wrapper of the card's own, so the rule
above does not reach it and the consumer centres the loader there.

## Reference

### Props

| Prop | Type | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `noun` | `string` | The subject's icon, resolved by `Icon`. Defaults to `"fox"`. |
| `inline` | `boolean` | Renders the one-line size on a text line. Defaults to `false`. |
| `label` | `string` | The accessible name of the loading region. Defaults to `"Loading"`. |

## Accessibility

The loader is a live region: `role="status"` on the root, named by `label`. A consumer
that knows the subject passes a label that names it, because "Loading" alone tells a
screen-reader user which region is busy only when one region is.

The ring is decorative and hidden. The icon is not: it announces its noun, as `Icon` does
everywhere else in the system.

## Not shown here

**Reduced motion.** Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` the ring stops and the loader
stays legible as a static ring and icon. A book cannot emulate the query for one subtree
without duplicating the animation, and a demonstration that fakes it teaches the wrong
thing, so the behaviour is covered by `apps/design/e2e/cn-loader.spec.ts`.

**Inside a button.** The inline loader is meant for a command in flight, which needs the
button's own loading state. That state is not specified yet, so the composition is absent
from this book until it is.

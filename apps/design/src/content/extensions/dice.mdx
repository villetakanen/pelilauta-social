Use Dice to render a die denomination, rolled result, or target number inline within running text.

## When to use it

Use Dice for inline tabletop rules notation in rules text, stat blocks, item descriptions, and difficulty checks. In wiki documents, write [wiki dice notation](/docs/10-wikisyntax) rather than hand-authoring raw markup.

Do not use Dice for large standalone display dice, interactive dice rolling widgets, or non-polyhedral game dice.

## Typographic integration

A die face is a touch taller than the line it sits in — `--cn-line` × 7/6, the same ratio `cn-dice`'s inline default uses — and aligns with the surrounding text baseline. It sits directly beside punctuation and inline links without disrupting paragraph leading or measure.

## Visual encoding

Every denomination has its own silhouette and its own colour. The seven dice run along the warning family, from `--chroma-warning-90` (d2) to `--chroma-warning-40` (d20); each takes one of five equal lightness steps between them, so no two dice share a colour:

- **d2 (Coin):** Round silhouette, warning-90, dark text
- **d4 (Triangle):** Triangle silhouette, 1/6 toward warning-40, dark text
- **d6 (Square):** Square silhouette, 2/6 toward warning-40, dark text
- **d8 (Diamond):** Diamond silhouette, 3/6 toward warning-40, light text
- **d10 (Kite / Gem):** Six-sided gem silhouette, 4/6 toward warning-40, light text
- **d12 (Decagon):** Ten-sided silhouette, 5/6 toward warning-40, light text
- **d20 (Tilted Diamond):** Tilted diamond silhouette, warning-40, light text

Text colour is the stronger of white or black per die, clearing WCAG 2.2 AA (4.5:1) against its face in the default warning family. d8 is the exception: white on d8 measures 3.37:1, below the 4.5:1 floor — an operator-ruled deviation, not an oversight. Every other face clears AA. The ramp sits in the page's mid-tone band on purpose, so every face also separates from a light page and a dark one, not only from its own text.

### Legibility against a tight silhouette

The face glyph is sized to fit its silhouette, not the reverse: one character reads at prose size, two characters step down. d2 is the smallest shape (85% of the box) among the one-character faces, and d10 is the narrowest waist among the two-character ones — the tightest fit at each size.

## Reference

### Attributes

| Attribute | Values | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `data-sides` | `2 \| 4 \| 6 \| 8 \| 10 \| 12 \| 20` | Required. Selects the die silhouette and colour step. |
| `data-value` | string | Required. The numeric face value. |
| `data-kind` | `die \| result \| target` | Required. Controls semantic styling and accessible naming. |
| `data-length` | `1 \| 2` | Required. Selects single-digit or two-digit font scaling. |
| `aria-label` | string | Required. The accessible name announced to assistive technology. |
| `role` | `img` | Required. Identifies the span as an image. |

These guarantees hold only for the default warning family, and d8's exception still leaves its silhouette to identify the die. Custom themes must verify contrast separately.

## Accessibility

The element emits `role="img"`. The author must supply `aria-label` with the full denomination and value (e.g. `[d20, 20+]`), as the element presents visual iconography.

d8 is a known exception to the contrast guarantee: white text on its face measures 3.37:1, below the 4.5:1 AA floor, an operator-ruled deviation rather than an oversight. Every other die's text clears AA against its face.

This is the lexicon: what the colour tokens are and what each one is worth. Which one to
reach for, and the contrast rules that constrain the choice, are in the
[Colour System](/principles/color-system) book.

Every table is read from its stylesheet when this page is built. Swatches resolve through
the live cascade, so each one shows the arm of `light-dark()` that matches the mode you
are reading in — switch your system theme and the same page shows the other.

## Semantic roles

What a colour is *for*. Each states Light and Dark as the two arms of one `light-dark()`
declaration, so there is one place a role is defined and no second theme to keep in step.
These are what a component reads.

## Elevation and shadows

Declared in the same stylesheet, but they are shadows rather than colours, so they carry
no swatch. They are grid multiples and resolve only where `--cn-grid` is defined, which is
why `styles/tokens.css` composes both families — see
[Units and grid](/tokens/units).

## The reference palette

The literal colours, in OKLCH, where the stack bottoms out. A component should not read
these — they say what is available, not what it means.

## Cyan 4 aliases are not here

`styles/compat/cyan-4.css` maps legacy names onto these roles until the surfaces still
running on Cyan are replaced. It is removed before `v21.0.0-rc.1`, nothing new reads it,
and it is not published as vocabulary. Read the stylesheet if you are migrating a surface;
`docs/MIGRATION.md` owns the sweep.

## What this does not cover

Contrast figures, the guardrails they produce, and which role to reach for are the
[Colour System](/principles/color-system) book's. Spacing, radii and icon sizes are in
[Units and grid](/tokens/units).

One shadow token sits outside the `--cn-shadow` family — `--cn-reply-dock-shadow` — and
appears in the semantic table, because that is where it is declared.

Pelilauta is text: discussion threads, character sheets, the shared library.
[Lato](/base/fonts) sets all of it, and Roboto Mono sets code. Every size is a step on one closed scale, an
Augmented Fourth built on the reading size — and the reading size is the reader's,
taken from their browser, never set by the system. Hierarchy comes from document
structure: write the element, and it renders on the scale.

## Hierarchy

Ordinary markup is on the scale with no class at all: `body` reads at the text step,
and `h1` through `h4` read theirs. Choose the heading level by document structure —
what section the heading opens — and the size follows.

A heading that looks too large for its spot is a layout question, not a reason to
write an `h3` where the document needs an `h2`. In a narrow container it
[steps down on its own](#the-downshift).

A step's size, line, weight and tracking are one decision. Overriding any of them — a
heavier `h2`, a tightened line — takes the text off the system and breaks the
[rhythm](#rhythm). Emphasis inside a step is `strong`, which renders at the emphasis
weight.

The step set is closed, and [Typography tokens](/tokens/typography) publishes every
value. Needing a size between two steps means changing the typography spec, not
writing a `font-size`.

## Step classes

Four steps have no element of their own and ship as classes:

| Class | Step | Reach for it when |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `.cn-text-title` | title | One display line at page scale — a front page, a landing. |
| `.cn-text-small` | small | Prose too secondary for reading size and too long for caption. |
| `.cn-text-caption` | caption | Metadata — a timestamp, a byline, a count. |
| `.cn-text-label` | label | A UI label — a column header, a form label, a badge. |

Small is in the family without being on the scale: it is not an Augmented Fourth from
anything, and it exists for running text where caption is too small to read at length
and reading size claims too much weight.

## Labels and captions

Label and caption share the caption size and line; the choice between them is casing.
A label uppercases whatever it is given and adds weight and tracking. A caption
renders its content as written.

<p><span class="cn-text-label">Keskiviikon peli-ilta</span><br /><span class="cn-text-caption">Keskiviikon peli-ilta</span></p>

The same content, as a label and as a caption. Finnish compounds are long and lose
their word shape in capitals, which works on a column header and fails on a
sentence. Content whose casing carries meaning — a name, a title someone wrote — is
not a label.

## Rhythm

Every step's line is a multiple of the layout's line unit, `--cn-line`, so text set
anywhere in the interface shares one grid with the layout around it —
[Spatial System](/principles/spatial-system) explains the unit.

Two consequences:

- Resizing a step without recomputing its line breaks the rhythm everywhere the step
  appears.
- Headings ship no margins. Whatever owns the layout states the spacing — in a
  [content container](/base/content-containers), the column's row-gap. Adding a
  margin to a heading double-spaces it there.

## The downshift

Every heading from `h1` to `h4` renders one step down where its container is narrower
than the small-screen breakpoint. The query measures the container rather than the
window, so a heading in a narrow card steps down whatever the page around it is doing.
Each heading keeps its own weight on the way down, except `h4`, whose lower step is
reading size and takes the emphasis weight to keep reading as a heading. The title
step does not downshift.

The call site writes nothing — no responsive classes, no media queries — but anything
that declares `container-type: inline-size` takes a constraint. A container is a
containing block for absolutely and fixed positioned descendants, and a new stacking
context. A FAB, tray or dock positioned against the viewport inside one renders
against the container instead: visible, correctly styled, wrongly placed. Position
viewport chrome outside the containers.

## The reading size

Every step is stated in `rem`, and nothing in the system sets the document's own text
size. A reader who asks their browser for larger text gets the whole scale
proportionally larger.

One declaration defeats this: a `font-size` on `html` or `:root` replaces the
reader's preference with a number of ours, and every step is measured against it.
None is written, anywhere.

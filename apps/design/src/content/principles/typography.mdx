Typography at Pelilauta Social bridges human conversation and technical clarity. We
rely on two complementary typefaces: Lato provides an approachable, highly readable
voice for UI elements, headings, and community content, while Roboto Mono brings
structure, precision, and clarity to code snippets, metadata, and systemic data.

<div class="surface elevation-1">

### Lato

<p class="text-subtitle">Warmth, Clarity, and Universal Scale</p>

Lato's semi-rounded details and warm curves ensure legibility on all screens while
avoiding a clinical feel. Designed by Łukasz Dziedzic, its balanced proportions
create a friendly presence in community discussions and UI labels. At larger
weights, Lato's character defines headings, and its high x-height maintains clarity
in both light and dark modes.

### Roboto Mono

<p class="text-subtitle">Precision, Structure, and Technical Rigor</p>

Designed by Christian Robertson, Roboto Mono forms the backbone for system
mechanics, data, and developer features. Its monospaced grid ensures code, logs, and
metadata align predictably. Optimized for screens, Roboto Mono uses clear
letterforms—like disambiguated zeros and ones—to prevent confusion when reading or
copying technical content.

## Type Scale & Vertical Rhythm

Our typographic system starts with a 17px body baseline at regular weight 400,
ensuring legibility and consistency. All text aligns to the 8px grid, while a
primary line height of 24px (three times the grid) maintains ideal line length and a
strong, consistent rhythm for multi-line content.

### Modular Scale

To step above and below our baseline, we apply a modular scale multiplier of 1:1.414 (Augmented Fourth).

Mathematically derived from the square root of two (√2 ≈ 1.414), an Augmented Fourth is an expressive, medium-high contrast scale. In UI applications, subtle scales (like a 1.25 Major Third) often fail to create enough distinction between section headers and page titles, while steep scales (like a 1.618 Golden Ratio) escalate too rapidly, consuming excess screen real estate on mobile viewports.

The Augmented Fourth strikes the ideal balance for Pelilauta Social:

* Clear Visual Hierarchy: It provides distinct, intentional jumps between text tiers. Headings naturally dominate the page, guiding the user's eye effortlessly without requiring extra-bold font weights to force emphasis.
* Balanced Density: It allows display sizes (h1, h2) to feel impactful and authoritative while maintaining a tight, readable footprint that fits dense, community-driven interfaces.
* Predictable Proportions: Because 1.414² = 2, every two steps up the scale cleanly doubles the font size (17px text → 24px h4 → 34px h3), creating an underlying mathematical harmony across complex layout compositions.

### The Steps

We provide an eight-step scale, from 12px captions to a 96px display title.

The −1 step (small text) is manually adjusted and sits outside the mathematical

## Container-Aware Scale Adaptation

A steep scale comes with a structural trade-off: a 48px H2 that cleanly anchors a full page layout completely overwhelms a compact card component. Because over half of Pelilauta’s headings live within cards, list rows, and preview feeds, our typography scale adapts dynamically to its local layout context.

Every heading tier from H1 down to H4 automatically renders one step lower on the scale whenever its parent container falls below our small-screen breakpoint:
* Container-Relative Scaling: We enforce this behavior using CSS Container Queries (@container) rather than viewport queries (@media). Because a narrow card requires card-proportional headings regardless of global page width, measuring the container guarantees correct visual hierarchy inside sidebars, multi-column grids, and nested modules alike.
* Proportional Layout Harmony: Downscaling by a single modular step preserves the exact same <code>1.414</code> ratio between heading levels while keeping line lengths and padding inside tight components balanced and readable.

Our colour system pairs a cool cerulean surface palette with a primary spectrum
transitioning from teal to yellow-green. Surface tones anchor layout backgrounds
and panels, while primary tones signify navigation, interactive focus, and key actions.
Dedicated functional families provide unambiguous feedback for errors, warnings,
and social reactions; informational notices are a semantic role over primary.

Light and Dark themes are independent expressions of this unified semantic model.
Both themes share identical semantic tokens and elevation tiers.

## Layered colour architecture

We structure colour declarations in three decoupled tiers:
1. **Reference scales:** Primitive OKLCH palette ramps defining available tones.
2. **Semantic roles:** Theme-aware tokens mapping reference tones to UI intent (`light-dark()`).
3. **Component mappings:** UI components consume semantic roles, and reach the palette
   only for decoration.

This separation allows reference palettes to be tuned without breaking component styling.
Complete token definitions are published in [Colour tokens](/tokens/color).

Overlays are roles as much as opaque surfaces are. A translucent surface, a wash and a
scrim each say something about the page, so each is named and themed with the rest.

Decoration is the exception. Where a colour states nothing — a gradient's stops, the
flourish the theme switch throws — a component takes a palette step and needs no role for
it.

## Perceptual OKLCH scales

All primitive colour scales are declared in the OKLCH colour space. Step numbers represent
perceptual lightness: step 40 exhibits identical perceived lightness across all hues.
Steps 0 and 100 represent neutral absolute endpoints.

The surface family maintains a constant hue so step transitions read purely as lightness
changes. The primary family shifts hue across its scale—from teal at dark steps to
yellow-green at light steps—providing visual vibrancy for interactive elements.

### Contrast by construction

Because OKLCH step numbers record uniform perceived lightness, the numerical delta
between two palette steps establishes a verified lower bound for contrast ratio:

| Step difference | Minimum contrast ratio | WCAG compliance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 45 or more | 3:1 | WCAG AA Large Text |
| 60 or more | 4.5:1 | WCAG AA Normal Text |
| 70 or more | 7:1 | WCAG AAA Enhanced |

A step difference of 40 provides no guarantee; the lowest measured ratio among those pairs
is 2.36:1.

Theme mappings preserve intent rather than raw numeric symmetry. In Light theme,
the ground plane uses surface step 95 while levels 1–3 use pure white. In Dark theme,
levels 1 and 2 share surface step 30 while level 3 increases lightness. Level 4
transitions to the primary family in both themes.

## Surface and elevation

Elevation establishes depth across five distinct tiers:
- **Level 0:** Application ground plane.
- **Level 1:** Resting content surfaces (cards, panels).
- **Level 2:** Content raised above resting surfaces.
- **Level 3:** Floating contextual overlays (menus, popovers).
- **Level 4:** Highest system layer (modal dialogs).

### Light and shadow interaction

In Light theme, levels 1 through 3 share a single white background; visual separation
is achieved through shadow geometry. In Dark theme, levels 2 and 3 separate through both
lightness differences and ambient shadows.

Levels 0 and 1 are shadowless. Nested surfaces rising by one level remain shadowless;
larger level increases render relative shadows scaled to the ancestor delta to prevent
excessive shadow accumulation.

### System layer surfaces

Level 4 surfaces use primary step 99 in Light and primary step 40 in Dark. Default body
text (`--cn-text`) meets WCAG 2.2 AA contrast on level 4 across both themes.

For shadow measurements, see [Spatial System](/principles/spatial-system). For utility
class usage, see [Surface reference](/base/surface).

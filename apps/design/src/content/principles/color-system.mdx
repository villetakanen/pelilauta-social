Pelilauta combines a cool cerulean surface palette with a primary palette that
moves from teal to an acid yellow-green. The surface family carries most of the
interface; primary colour identifies navigation, focus, and high-emphasis actions.
Error, warning, information, and love retain distinct hues for functional and
social signals.

Dark and Light are two expressions of this system. We design in Dark, then review
Light as a complete theme. Both preserve the same semantic roles and elevation
hierarchy even where their palette steps differ.

## Layered colour model

We separate the system into three layers:

1. Reference scales define the colours available to the system.
2. Semantic roles assign purpose and provide one value for each theme.
3. Components consume roles according to meaning.

This direction lets the palette evolve without changing the purpose expressed by
a component. A link continues to consume `--cn-link`; the semantic layer decides
which reference colour represents a link in each theme. The complete declarations
are in [Colour tokens](/tokens/color).

## Perceptual scales

The reference palette is expressed in OKLCH. A step number records perceived
lightness: step 40 has the same lightness across every family, while chroma and hue
determine its colour. Steps 0 and 100 are neutral endpoints.

The surface and primary families provide the full tonal range needed for themes,
text, borders, and interaction states.

Surface holds one hue so changes in its step read primarily as changes in
lightness. Primary rotates through the scale, moving from teal at the dark end to
yellow-green at the light end. This rotation gives interactive colour a stronger
identity than the surfaces supporting it.

### Functional colour

Functional families provide the four tonal positions used by current semantic
roles. Their separate hues keep destructive action, caution, system information,
and social affirmation visually distinct.

### Contrast by construction

Reference steps are selected when defining a semantic role. Components continue
to consume the role rather than choosing a palette step directly. Because the step
records lightness, the distance between two steps provides a tested lower bound
for their contrast across the palette.

| Step difference | Minimum contrast   |
| :-------------- | :----------------- |
| 45              | WCAG AA Large, 3:1 |
| 60              | WCAG AA, 4.5:1     |
| 70              | WCAG AAA, 7:1      |

A difference of 40 provides no WCAG guarantee; the lowest measured ratio among
those pairs is 2.36:1.

Theme mappings preserve purpose rather than numerical symmetry. The Light ground
plane uses surface step 95, while levels 1 to 3 use white. Dark gives levels 1 and
2 the same background, then increases lightness at level 3. Level 4 uses the
primary family in both themes. Link colour comes from the primary family in Light
and the surface family in Dark, while its role remains navigation in both.

## Surface and elevation

Elevation establishes the visual order between the page, its content, and elements
temporarily raised above that content. We express it through five levels:

- Level 0 is the application ground plane.
- Level 1 holds resting content such as cards and panels.
- Level 2 raises content above a resting surface.
- Level 3 holds floating or active content such as menus and popovers.
- Level 4 is reserved for the highest system layer, including dialogs.

### Colour and shadow

Colour and shadow work together at every level. Light uses one white background
for levels 1 to 3, so shadow provides most of their separation. Dark distinguishes
levels 2 and 3 through both lightness and shadow. The shadows derive from the same
grid as the surrounding spacing, keeping their scale proportional to the
composition.

Levels 0 and 1 are shadowless. A nested surface also remains shadowless when it
rises by one level; larger rises use a shadow relative to the nearest elevated
ancestor. This prevents a nested composition from accumulating the full shadow of
every absolute level.

### The highest layer

Level 4 uses primary step 99 in Light and primary step 50 in Dark, separating the
highest system layer from the surface hierarchy. `--cn-text-high` is the general
foreground for this level. Components that use another foreground must verify its
contrast against `--cn-surface-4` in both themes.

The [Spatial System](/principles/spatial-system) defines the shadow measurements.
The [Surface reference](/base/surface) documents the classes and their composition.

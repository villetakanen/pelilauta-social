Pick a **role**, never a step. Roles carry both modes; a palette step carries one, so
reaching past the role gets Light right and Dark wrong. Everything below is about the
scales underneath, because knowing how they are built is what makes the roles predictable
— and what tells you when they run out.

## A step number is a lightness

Every colour is `oklch(L C H)`, and **the step is L × 100**. `surface-50` is `oklch(0.5 …)`.
Nothing else in the system is numbered that way, and it is the property everything here
rests on.

Chroma is not constant along a ramp: it peaks in the middle and falls to zero at both
ends, because a near-black and a near-white cannot hold saturation. That is why step 0 and
step 100 are neutral in every family.

## The gap between two steps predicts their contrast

Because the step is the lightness, the **distance** between two steps in the same family
puts a floor under their contrast. Measured across every same-family pair in the palette:

| Step gap | Guarantees at least |
| :--- | :--- |
| 45 | AA Large — 3:1 |
| **60** | **AA — 4.5:1** |
| 70 | AAA — 7:1 |

So `surface-95` on `surface-20` is a gap of 75 and safe without checking. A gap of 40
guarantees nothing — its worst pair is 2.36:1 — which is the trap, because 40 feels like a
lot of scale.

These are floors over the whole palette, not predictions: a given pair usually does better.
They are asserted in `test/color-contrast.test.ts`, so the rule cannot drift from the
colours.

## Surface holds its hue. Primary does not.

Every family fixes one hue for its whole ramp — surface at 242, error 318, warning 65,
info 170, love 30 — **except primary, which rotates from 185 at the bottom to 110 at the
top.** Dark primary is a blue-teal; light primary is a yellow-green. They are ends of one
ramp, not two colours.

The consequence is practical: a primary step chosen in Light and its counterpart in Dark
are different hues, not the same hue at different brightness. Surface has no such problem,
which is why elevation can be built from it.

Primary and surface run thirteen steps, 0 to 100. The four status families run **four** —
20, 40, 60, 90 — so there is no `error-30`, and the gap rule is the only way to pick
between the ones that exist.

## Light and Dark are two expressions, not an inversion

One declaration states both, using `light-dark()`. There is no theme class and no
JavaScript; `styles/preflight.css` declares `color-scheme`, and the browser picks the arm.

It would be easy to assume Dark is the palette flipped — step *n* becoming step *100 − n*.
It is not. Of 41 roles that state both arms, **29 are not mirror steps and 4 change family
outright**:

- `--cn-surface-1` through `-3` are pure white in Light, but `surface-30`–`40` in Dark,
  which is nowhere near their mirror.
- `--cn-link` is `primary-50` in Light and `surface-80` in Dark — a different family, not a
  lighter brand colour.
- Elevation rises in Dark (20 → 50) but **falls at the top in Light**: `--cn-surface-4` is
  `surface-80`, darker than the white beneath it.

That last one is the whole reason the next section exists.

## Where the scale runs out

Raised Dark surfaces climb towards mid-Cerulean, so each step up spends the headroom a
de-emphasised text colour needs. At elevation 4 there is none left:

**Use `--cn-text` or `--cn-text-high`, and nothing else.** `--cn-on-surface` — the ordinary
body role everywhere else — reaches only AA Large here and is not enough for body copy.

noticed. The practical form: a card that raises itself to elevation 4 cannot keep the
timestamp-and-byline treatment it used at rest.

## Choosing

- **Text** is `--cn-text`; `--cn-text-low` only for genuinely secondary content, never on a
  raised Dark surface.
- **Action** is `--cn-link` to navigate, `--cn-button` to commit. Brand colour marks a
  brand-affirming action, not a destructive one — that is `error`.
- **Status** is `error`, `warning`, `info`, and `love` for social approval. A like is not
  an error, however red it looks.
- **Focus** is `--cn-focus-ring`, and it may not be suppressed.

Do not override a role locally. If `--cn-link` is wrong on some surface, either the role or
the surface is wrong; setting `color` to a step in one component fixes one mode and breaks
the other.

## What this does not cover

Values are in the [Colour](/tokens/color) lexicon. The palette is not themeable at runtime
— Cyan 4 allowed swapping the chroma ramps, and v21 has no consumer for that. The
per-account theme preference in `apps/pelilauta` is application state, outside this system.

The colour system carries Pelilauta's identity coherently through every surface. A
site owner replaces that identity by supplying a chroma family — a rebrand
without a redesign — while every reader keeps the same hierarchy, states, and
contrast in Light and Dark. `specs/design-system/color-system/spec.md` governs the
contract; this book argues its shape.

## Chroma: the theme-supplied layer

Chroma families are literal, lightness-indexed OKLCH scales: `--chroma-{family}-{step}`,
step 0 black to step 100 white. The step number states perceived lightness, so equal
steps carry equal lightness across every family — a replacement theme keeps that
guarantee by supplying a complete family rather than individual steps.

The primary family shifts hue as it lightens: petrol and teal at the dark end,
yellow-green at the light end. The rotation mimics how colour behaves under real
light — the deep greens of a northern forest as the sun reaches them, glowing sunlit
yellow where the light lands and shadowed green where it does not. A single held hue
would be a swatch; the rotation is a landscape.

<ColorRamp
  theme={theme}
  family="primary"
  intent="Links, keyboard focus rings, and high-emphasis controls."
/>

The surface family holds a constant cerulean hue and varies only in lightness, giving
the architecture depth and atmosphere rather than neutral chrome.

<ColorRamp
  theme={theme}
  family="surface"
  intent="Backgrounds, elevation surfaces, borders, and body text."
/>

Two core families, primary and surface, are the only themeable surface: complete
13-step scales, and a replacement theme changes no semantic or component token.

### The auxiliary status families

Three auxiliary families are fixed, never themeable: love, warning, and error. A
status colour is a learned convention rather than a brand signal, and a convention's
value is staying put — a re-brandable error colour would break the low-cognition
recognition it exists for. Love is a structural family, not decoration: reactions are
the community's main social currency, and appreciation is a first-class signal.

Each auxiliary family carries only the four jobs a status colour has: dark text on a
tint, a strong fill for Light, a bright fill for Dark, and a pale tint background. That
is why the scale is a partial 20/40/60/90, not the core families' 13 steps.

<ColorRamp
  theme={theme}
  family="error"
  intent="Destructive actions and validation failures."
/>
<ColorRamp
  theme={theme}
  family="warning"
  intent="Cautionary feedback and alerts."
/>
<ColorRamp

  family="love"
  intent="Likes, bookmarks, and community appreciation."
/>

Information is not a fourth family. `--cn-color-info` and `--cn-color-on-info` are a semantic pair
over primary — the v20 info scale named a chroma distinction the design never
actually made.

### Contrast by construction

Because OKLCH step numbers record uniform perceived lightness, the numerical delta
between two palette steps establishes a verified lower bound for contrast ratio:

| Step difference | Minimum contrast ratio | WCAG compliance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 45 or more | 3:1 | WCAG AA Large Text |
| 60 or more | 4.5:1 | WCAG AA Normal Text |
| 70 or more | 7:1 | WCAG AAA Enhanced |

A step difference of 40 provides no guarantee; the lowest measured ratio among those
pairs is 2.36:1. `packages/design-system/test/color-contrast.test.ts` computes these
floors from the palette; this table cites them rather than restating a copy.

### A theme swap, live

<DsComposition caption="Each toggle redeclares one core family's 13 steps on this container alone; the sample card and both ramps read them live">
  <ThemeSwapDemo client:load />
</DsComposition>

A theme swap is not a filter over the rendered page — it is a core family's thirteen
custom properties, redeclared. Primary and surface toggle independently, each
replacing its own `--chroma-{family}-0` through `--chroma-{family}-100` with a
zero-chroma ramp that keeps every step's OKLCH lightness, so the card's heading,
link, button and ground all read the same hierarchy and contrast as before; only the
hue left. That is the rebrand-without-a-redesign the colour system promises: a site
owner supplies new primary and surface families, and every semantic role built on
them re-themes without a single component changing.

## Semantics: the stable component-facing layer

Semantic roles name purpose under `--cn-*`. A scheme-dependent role states Light and
Dark as two arms of one `light-dark()` declaration; a scheme-invariant role states one
expression. Components consume a role for its purpose, never a chroma step directly —
so a theme change moves every role together, links included, and nothing is
overridden one at a time.

A role's mapping preserves intent rather than raw numeric symmetry. In Light, the
ground plane uses surface step 95 while levels 1 through 3 use pure white. In Dark, the
levels below the system layer span surface step 20 to step 40: the ground plane at step
20, levels 1 and 2 a third of the way to step 40, and level 3 two thirds of the way. Level 4 leaves the surface family for primary in both themes. Complete
token declarations are published in [Colour tokens](/tokens/color).

## Decoration: the chroma exception

Where a colour states nothing — a gradient's stops, the flourish the theme switch
throws — a component takes a palette step directly and needs no role for it. This is
the one place a component reaches past the semantic layer, and it is named exactly
because it is the exception: a purpose the system has named always uses that role
instead.

## Transparency: the ladder of shares

Before v19, a layer that let something through picked a share by eye, so two
translucent layers on one page carried no relation to each other. The transparency
ladder makes a share a choice from a fixed set instead: eleven-percent steps,
`--cn-transparency-1` through `--cn-transparency-9`, with a half step below the
smallest. A component that needs translucency takes a rung; it does not compute one.

<DsComposition caption="Every rung, each mixing the primary role over a two-tone backdrop">
  <TransparencyLadder source={transparency} />
</DsComposition>

A rung promises nothing about contrast: the layer beneath a translucent surface is not
always the swatch backdrop above, and where it is unknown — artwork behind a poster —
no percentage can guarantee a ratio. Opacity is the one share that could guarantee a
ratio, and the ladder removes opacity as a choice. Legibility over unknown artwork
comes from which levels cede and how far, a decision `specs/design-system/surface/spec.md`
states; see [Surface reference](/base/surface).

## Surface and elevation

Elevation establishes depth across five distinct tiers:
- **Level 0:** The application itself.
- **Level 1:** Payload, the default.
- **Level 2:** Payload inside payload, reached by nesting rather than chosen.
- **Level 3:** Floats over content, whether it stays or is dismissed.
- **Level 4:** A system interrupt.

### Light and shadow interaction

Light and Dark separate the levels by different means. In Light, levels 1 through 3
share a single white background, so shadow alone carries the separation. In Dark, level
1 steps up from the application and level 3 steps up again, because a shadow carries
less on a dark ground than on a light one. Levels 1 and 2 share a background in both
themes, and their shadows separate them.

Levels 0 and 1 are shadowless. Nested surfaces rising by one level remain shadowless;
larger level increases render relative shadows scaled to the ancestor delta to prevent
excessive shadow accumulation.

### System layer surfaces

Level 4 surfaces use primary step 99 in Light and primary step 40 in Dark. Default body
text (`--cn-color-text`) meets WCAG 2.2 AA contrast on level 4 across both themes.

For shadow measurements, see [Spatial System](/principles/spatial-system). For utility
class usage, see [Surface reference](/base/surface).

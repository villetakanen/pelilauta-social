Colour in Pelilauta is decided once, as a role, and expressed twice, as Light and Dark.
A component that reads a role gets both modes for free and keeps working when the
expression changes. A component that reaches past the role into a palette step gets one
mode right and the other wrong.

Values are in the [Colour](/tokens/color) lexicon. This book is the choosing.

## Three layers, and consumers read the middle one

**Reference** colours are the literal palette in OKLCH — thirteen steps of primary and
surface, four each of error, warning, info and love. They say what exists. Nothing in a
component should name one.

**Semantic** roles say what a colour is *for*: `--cn-text`, `--cn-link`, `--cn-surface-2`,
`--cn-border-focus`. Each is one declaration whose two `light-dark()` arms point at
different reference steps. This is the layer to read.

**Compatibility** aliases carry Cyan 4's names onto v21 roles so unmigrated v18 code keeps
working. Temporary, and shrinking. Nothing new is written against it.

The direction is one-way: semantic roles are built from reference colours, compatibility
from semantic. A value never travels back up.

## The perceptual palette is why the steps behave

The palette is OKLCH, where the first number is perceived lightness. `surface-50` is half
as light as white to the eye, not merely half by some channel arithmetic. That is what
makes a step predictable: moving from `surface-20` to `surface-30` changes apparent
lightness by the same amount everywhere in the palette, in every hue.

It also means **a colour's step number predicts its contrast**, which is what the
guardrails below rest on. Derived colours — hover overlays, tints — must therefore mix in
the same space: `color-mix(in oklch, …)`, never `in hsl`, or the perceptual spacing that
makes the step numbers meaningful is lost in the mix.

## Surfaces get lighter as they rise, and dark mode is where that bites

Elevation runs `--cn-surface` through `--cn-surface-4`. In Light mode the surfaces move
towards white as they rise; in Dark they move *away* from black, towards a mid Cerulean.
So the higher a dark surface, the less room a de-emphasised text colour has.

## The elevation-4 guardrail

At the top of the scale that headroom runs out. These figures are computed from the
production stylesheets when this page builds, and asserted in
`packages/design-system/test/color-contrast.test.ts`:

<ContrastMatrix
  foregrounds={['--cn-text', '--cn-text-high', '--cn-on-surface', '--cn-text-low', '--cn-on-surface-secondary']}
  background="--cn-surface-4"
  modes={['dark']}
  caption="On --cn-surface-4 in Dark mode"
/>

**On a dark elevation-4 surface, use `--cn-text` or `--cn-text-high` and nothing else.**
`--cn-text-low` and `--cn-on-surface-secondary` fail outright. `--cn-on-surface` — the
ordinary body-text role everywhere else — lands in AA Large territory and is not
sufficient for body copy.

The practical consequence: a card that raises itself to elevation 4 cannot carry the
timestamp-and-byline treatment it used at rest. Either it stays at a lower elevation, or
its secondary text is promoted.

  <p style="color: var(--cn-text); margin: 0;">This is `--cn-text` on elevation 4 — the role to use.</p>
  <p style="color: var(--cn-text-low); margin: 0;">This is `--cn-text-low` on elevation 4 — the role the guardrail forbids.</p>

<small>In Light mode both lines are legible, which is exactly why the rule has to be
stated rather than noticed.</small>

## Reach for the role that names the job

  source={themeSource}
  names={['--cn-text', '--cn-text-low', '--cn-text-high', '--cn-link', '--cn-border', '--cn-focus-ring']}
  swatch
  caption="The roles reached for most often"
/>

- **Text** is `--cn-text`. Drop to `--cn-text-low` only for genuinely secondary content,
  and never on a raised dark surface.
- **Action** is `--cn-link` for navigation and `--cn-button` for a committed action.
  Brand colour marks a brand-affirming action; it does not mark a destructive one.
- **Status** is `error`, `warning`, `info` — and `love` for social approval. A like is not
  an error, however red it looks.
- **Focus** is `--cn-focus-ring`, always. It is the one role that may not be suppressed:
  a keyboard reader who cannot see focus cannot use the page.

Do not override a semantic role locally. If `--cn-link` is wrong for a surface, the role's
definition is wrong or the surface is; setting `color` to a palette step in one component
fixes one mode and breaks the other.

## What this does not cover

Token values, including the full role list, are in the [Colour](/tokens/color) lexicon.
The persisted per-account theme preference in `apps/pelilauta` is application state and
sits outside this contract — the design system reads only the browser or system setting
through `color-scheme`, which `styles/preflight.css` owns.

`.surface` provides the standard container for bounded content, applying default inset
and establishing a container query context. Five accompanying elevation utilities
(`.elevation-0` through `.elevation-4`) control visual depth and shadow geometry.

Use `.surface` when content requires standard padding and a local query context. Apply
an elevation utility directly when an existing element requires only visual elevation.

## Container model

`.surface` applies standard content inset and establishes the `surface-area` query
container. Descendant components evaluate container queries against the surface's
computed inline size rather than the viewport. Default presentation is elevation 1.

| Property | Value |
| :--- | :--- |
| `padding` | `--cn-gap` |
| `container` | `surface-area / inline-size` |
| Default elevation | 1 |

The inset remains `--cn-gap` across all viewports. Compositions requiring custom inset
must declare it explicitly.

## Elevation levels

Each elevation utility applies the background color role and box shadow assigned to
that level. Elevation hierarchy is defined in [Colour & Surface principles](/principles/color-system).

| Class | Background | Shadow | Level means | Under a poster |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `.elevation-0` | `--cn-color-surface` | none | The application itself | a wide share |
| `.elevation-1` | `--cn-color-surface-1` | none | Payload, the default | a narrow share |
| `.elevation-2` | `--cn-color-surface-2` | `--cn-shadow-elevation-2` | Payload inside payload | a narrow share |
| `.elevation-3` | `--cn-color-surface-3` | `--cn-shadow-elevation-3` | Floats over content | opaque |
| `.elevation-4` | `--cn-color-surface-4` | `--cn-shadow-elevation-4` | A system interrupt | opaque |

Elevation 0 renders an opaque ground plane, surface step 95 in Light and surface step
20 in Dark, except where a poster paints the page's background instead; see
[Under a poster](#under-a-poster). Elevations 0 and 1 are shadowless; elevations 2
through 4 apply progressively larger box shadows when rendered without an elevated
ancestor.

Levels 1 and 2 share a background in both schemes and separate by their shadows alone.
Level 1 stands away from the application: surface step 100 in Light, and in Dark a
third of the way from step 20 to step 40. Level 3 shares that background again in
Light, and in Dark stands two thirds of the way from step 20 to step 40 — as far above
level 2 as level 1 stands above the application — because a shadow carries less on a
dark ground than on a light one. Level 4 leaves the surface family for primary, step 99
in Light and step 40 in Dark.

Every level carries the inherited body foreground at WCAG 2.2 AA in both themes when it
paints its own background opaquely. Surface sets no foreground: a consumer raises one to
strengthen a role, not to repair it. De-emphasised roles are unsuitable at level 4.
Levels 0 through 2 lose that guarantee where a poster paints behind them, because
nothing can promise a ratio against artwork the system has never seen; see
[Under a poster](#under-a-poster).

## Under a poster

A poster replaces the page background with decorative artwork
([CnPoster](/components/cn-poster)). The canvas beneath it — the page's ground —
keeps painting level 0's colour opaquely, because the canvas is what the page is; the
poster paints over it and dissolves its lower edge back into that colour. The levels
standing on the artwork cede to it instead of staying opaque, each mixing its own
surface colour with a share taken from the [transparency ladder](/tokens/color):

- **Level 0**, the ground plane, takes a wide share (`--cn-transparency-6`), because a
  page standing over artwork is meant to show it.
- **Levels 1 and 2**, payload, take a narrow share (`--cn-transparency-2`): enough to
  admit the artwork and belong to the page it is on, while staying legible.
- **Levels 3 and 4** keep their opacity. They float over content rather than over the
  page, so ceding there would bleed whatever they cover.

Neither share responds to the artwork's contrast. [Colour System](/principles/color-system)
explains why a rung cannot promise one.

The cession applies only above the small-screen breakpoint, and withdraws — the poster
disappears and every level returns to its opaque colour — under
`prefers-reduced-transparency: reduce` or `forced-colors: active`. Below the breakpoint
the poster never renders, so no level cedes there either.

## Level overrides

To change visual elevation while preserving standard padding and container query context,
combine `.surface` with an explicit elevation class.

```html
<div class="surface elevation-3">A menu panel</div>
```

## Nested elevation

Nested elevation is calculated relative to the nearest elevated ancestor. The child element
retains its assigned background role, while its visible box shadow reflects only the level
difference between ancestor and child.

| Ancestor | Child | Visible shadow |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | 2 | none |
| 1 | 3 | `--cn-shadow-elevation-2` |
| 1 | 4 | `--cn-shadow-elevation-3` |
| 2 | 3 | none |
| 2 | 4 | `--cn-shadow-elevation-2` |
| 3 | 4 | none |

An elevation difference of +1 is shadowless. Differences of +2 or +3 render shadows
scaled to relative distance, preventing nested shadows from compounding into dark halos.

<DsComposition
  themes
  caption="Elevation 4 relative to ancestors at elevations 1 and 3"
>
  <div class="surface elevation-1">
    Ancestor at elevation 1
    <div class="surface elevation-4">Child at elevation 4</div>
  </div>
  <div class="surface elevation-3">
    Ancestor at elevation 3
    <div class="surface elevation-4">Child at elevation 4</div>
  </div>
</DsComposition>

`.surface` evaluates as elevation 1 by default in nesting calculations. When combined
with an explicit elevation class, the declared level applies.

## Attention flags

`has-notify` and `has-alert` render a triangular status flag in the upper-right corner
of any container. `has-notify` applies the information role, `has-alert` applies the
warning role, and `has-alert` takes precedence when both classes are present. The flag
matches the host element's corner radius and disables pointer events.

```html
<div class="surface has-notify">A row with unread content</div>
```

<DsComposition themes caption="A surface with each attention state">
  <div class="surface has-notify">Notification</div>
  <div class="surface has-alert">Alert</div>
</DsComposition>

Attention flags are utility classes rather than component-bound states. Components such
as [CnCard](/components/cn-card) compose `.has-notify` and `.has-alert` directly.

Client scripts can toggle attention flags on static, server-rendered DOM elements without
component hydration:

```ts
row.classList.toggle('has-notify', unread)
```

Attention flags are purely visual indicators and provide no screen reader announcements.
Applications must communicate unread or alert states through accessible text or ARIA live
regions.
